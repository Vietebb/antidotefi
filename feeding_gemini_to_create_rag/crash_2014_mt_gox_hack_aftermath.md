# Mt. Gox Hack & 2014 Crypto Crash: Event Timeline and Market Aftermath

## 1. Executive Summary
The collapse of Mt. Gox in February 2014 remains one of the most significant systemic shocks in cryptocurrency history. At its peak, the Tokyo-based exchange processed approximately 70% of all global Bitcoin (BTC) transactions. The revelation of a massive, multi-year exploit resulted in the loss of 850,000 BTC (750,000 customer funds, 100,000 corporate funds), triggering a severe liquidity crisis, a massive drawdown, and a multi-year bear market.

## 2. Event Timeline
*   **February 7, 2014:** Mt. Gox abruptly halts all Bitcoin withdrawals, citing anomalies in their wallet nodes. Panic selling begins on the platform, causing Mt. Gox BTC prices to decouple and trade at a massive discount compared to competitors like Bitstamp.
*   **February 10, 2014:** The exchange issues a press release blaming "transaction malleability"—a known flaw in the Bitcoin protocol that allows attackers to alter transaction hashes before confirmation, tricking the exchange into double-processing withdrawals.
*   **February 23, 2014:** Mt. Gox CEO Mark Karpelès resigns from the Bitcoin Foundation board. 
*   **February 24, 2014:** The Mt. Gox website goes completely offline. A leaked internal "Crisis Strategy Draft" reveals the exchange is insolvent and missing 744,408 BTC.
*   **February 28, 2014:** Mt. Gox officially files for bankruptcy protection in Tokyo, reporting $64 million in liabilities and essentially declaring the complete loss of customer deposits.

## 3. Price Impact and Drawdown Metrics
The event catalyzed a brutal macro-downtrend that defined the 2014-2015 crypto bear market.
*   **Immediate Crash:** On February 6, BTC traded at ~$850. By February 25, following the site closure, price crashed to ~$400—an immediate **-52.9%** drop across aggregate indices within 19 days.
*   **Peak-to-Trough Drawdown:** The macro top occurred prior to the collapse in late November 2013 at ~$1,150. The ultimate bear market bottom was reached on January 14, 2015, at ~$152. This represents a total peak-to-trough drawdown of **-86.7%**.
*   **Volume Profile:** Spot trading volumes cratered by over 60% globally as liquidity evaporated and retail participants exited the asset class.

## 4. Recovery Duration
The structural damage to market confidence resulted in a prolonged consolidation phase. 
*   **Days to Recovery:** It took precisely **1,181 days** (from the November 2013 peak) for Bitcoin to definitively reclaim and break its previous all-time high of $1,150. The breakout finally occurred on **February 23, 2017**.

## 5. Core Market Psychology & Microstructure
For algorithmic trading models, the Mt. Gox crash serves as the ultimate case study in **counterparty contagion and systemic risk**. 
*   **Flight to Cold Storage:** The psychology shifted instantly from speculative greed to extreme self-custody paranoia, birthing the maxim "not your keys, not your coins." 
*   **Order Book Illiquidity:** The sudden removal of 70% of global market making capability caused massive slippage and wildly fragmented pricing across surviving exchanges.
*   **Risk Premium Overcorrection:** The psychological scarring induced a permanent "exchange risk premium." Investors demanded significantly higher expected returns to justify holding capital on centralized platforms, depressing valuations for three consecutive years until institutional-grade custodians began entering the market in 2017.
